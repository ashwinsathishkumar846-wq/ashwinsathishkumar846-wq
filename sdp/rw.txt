182|Issue security permissions – Grouped into security schemes; they let the visibility of single issues be adjusted within the limits of the project's permissions.

324|Automation frees teams to concentrate on important work by taking over repeated manual tasks. An automation rule has three parts: a trigger that starts the rule, conditions that narrow it down, and actions that do the work. Triggers wait for events such as a work item being created or a field value changing. Conditions limit the scope of the rule, and the rule stops when a condition is not met. A rule can be Enabled, Disabled or in Draft, and each rule keeps an audit log that records when it ran and what the result was.

449|To understand sprint retrospective items in Jira.
450|Agile retrospectives are not "post-mortem" meetings held only when a project ends; they take place at the close of every sprint during the life of the project. A retrospective is central to the incremental value of a product and to the continuous improvement of a team. Without regular retrospectives, problems cannot be fixed before they grow, and teams miss the long-term benefits of Agile. In current Jira, retrospectives are run through a Confluence whiteboard or page that opens straight from Jira when a sprint is completed, so the retrospective stays linked to the real sprint data.
451|Set the Stage — builds a "safe space" for open sharing. Jira and Confluence are a natural venue because all the sprint information is already there and easy to refer to during the session.
452|Gather Data — team members add their ideas on their own instead of speaking one after another, which avoids "groupthink" where the first opinion shared influences everybody else.
453|Generate Insights — the team filters the ideas and votes on which topics matter most, deciding what to handle first. Voting can be set to limit the votes per person and per topic.
454|Decide What to Do — the most important stage and the one most often skipped. The result must be a concrete action item rather than a vague remark, and it should be made as a Jira work item so it can be tracked and followed up.
455|Close the Retrospective — the team agrees, formally or informally, on the action items and names an owner who makes sure each action gets done (not always the person who does the task).
456|Sign in to Jira and open the Scrum space you need.
457|Complete the active sprint (Board → Complete sprint), since retrospectives are normally triggered when a sprint closes.
458|When asked, choose the option to create a Sprint Retrospective in Confluence (as a whiteboard or a page).
459|Pick a whiteboard for real-time group input, or a page for a structured written retrospective.
460|Set the stage by inviting the team and briefly stating the purpose of the retrospective.
461|Gather data by letting each member add ideas or sticky notes on their own under headings such as "What went well," "What didn't go well" and "Ideas for improvement."
462|Generate insights by grouping similar ideas and voting for the topics most worth discussing.
463|Decide what to do by turning the top-voted insights into concrete action items and creating matching Jira work items to track them.
464|Name an owner for every action item.
465|Close the retrospective with the team's agreement and link it back to the completed sprint for future reference.
466|Thus sprint retrospective items were studied and demonstrated successfully in Jira.
467|To set up the Planning Poker integration in Jira using a Marketplace app.
468|Planning poker, also called "scrum poker" or "pointing poker," is a game-like estimation method in which a development team jointly guesses the effort needed for tasks. Estimates come from group agreement and not from one person's judgement, which makes them more engaging and statistically more accurate. Planning Poker is not built into Jira; it comes from Marketplace apps (such as Planning Poker for Jira, Actioned or similar tools) that connect to Jira through OAuth or application links, unlike the older manual "Application Links" setup of Server/Data Center versions.
469|Group estimates from planning poker are statistically more accurate than individual guesses.
470|Helps to estimate tasks relative to one another instead of in absolute units.
471|Brings out gaps between requirements and the understanding of the implementation early.
472|(Current Jira Cloud):
473|Sign in to Jira as an administrator.
474|Open the Atlassian Marketplace (Settings → Apps → Find new apps, or marketplace.atlassian.com directly).
475|Search for a Planning Poker app (for example, "Planning Poker for Jira").
476|Press Get it now / Try it free to add the app to your Jira site.
477|After the installation, open the app from the Apps menu in the top bar.
478|Follow the setup wizard inside the app to connect it to your Jira site; modern apps use OAuth authorisation instead of manually creating Application Links.
479|Grant the permissions it asks for (this takes the place of the older "Incoming Authentication" step).
480|Confirm that the connection is active by checking that the app's dashboard shows your Jira site as linked.
481|Move on to create a new estimation game (covered in Part 2).
482|Thus the Planning Poker integration was set up successfully in Jira.
483|To run a planning poker estimation session with a Planning Poker app in Jira.
484|Planning poker is a game-like technique that estimates project tasks through group agreement instead of individual estimates. Estimates become more accurate over time as planning poker apps refine them with continued use. Modern Planning Poker apps pull stories straight from Jira and send the point values back automatically when voting ends, so no manual data entry is needed.
485|Open the Planning Poker app from the Jira Apps menu (continuing from the setup in Part 1).
486|Authorise the app to reach your Jira site if that is not done yet, and press Allow when asked.
487|Press Create new game (or the equivalent) to begin a new estimation session.
488|Name the session and pick the Jira space or board from which work items will be imported.
489|Import the stories or tasks straight from the Jira backlog into the game.
490|Invite team members to the session with the game link or the in-app invite.
491|For every imported item, team members choose their estimates at the same time (the cards stay hidden until everyone has voted).
492|Reveal all the votes together to avoid bias and discuss any big differences in the estimates.
493|Vote again if required until the team agrees on a final estimate.
494|Confirm the final story point value; the app automatically writes it back to the Story point estimate field of the matching work item in Jira.
496|Thus planning poker estimation was conducted successfully in Jira.
